# Short-Notice Labour Mobility: Research Findings

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-09-29 |
| Method | Five parallel web-research passes (UK, US/NYC, France, Dubai + Hong Kong, platforms). Items marked *unverified* come from marketing or secondary sources. |
| Question | How fast and how strict is it really to get extra labour onto a site at short notice, and what legitimate fast routes exist? |
| Feeds | `00-business-model.md` §B.6 and §B.7 |

## 1. Headline

**The founder's instinct was right.** For basic work and individual workers, onboarding is not uniformly strict. Fast routes (same day to 3 days) exist in every market studied. The earlier claim of "1 to 4 weeks, so short-notice sharing isn't viable" applies only to **new company-level subcontract approvals**, not to workers.

**What's fast** (hours to 3 days): workers who **already hold** their site credentials, moved through **agencies / manpower suppliers**, **labour-only gangs**, or as a **sub-tier under a subcontractor already approved** on the destination site.

**What's slow** (1 to 6+ weeks): new company prequalification and client or consultant approval of a new subcontractor; credentials from scratch in the UK and Hong Kong.

**What's blocked:** licensed trades tied to a licence holder's payroll (e.g. NYC electrical and plumbing), union jobs, imported labour (Hong Kong), for-profit lending between companies outside agencies (France; Germany largely bans worker leasing into construction).

## 2. By market

| Market | Worker credential (portable) | Fastest legal routes | Slow gate | Blockers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UK / London** | CSCS card: industry rule (Build UK), not law; 1 to 3 weeks from scratch; induction 30 to 60 min | Agencies (same day, most within 24h); CIS labour-only gangs (online verification in seconds); sub-tier under an approved sub with written consent to sub-let | Company prequalification (Constructionline Gold / Common Assessment Standard) 3 to 6 weeks | False self-employment; **from 1 Oct 2026 illegal-working liability extends to agency workers and individual subcontractors, fines up to £60k per worker** (from a draft Home Office guide; confirm) |
| **US / NYC** | SST card (Local Law 196) where a site safety professional is required; **temporary card after about 2 days of OSHA 10**; 1 to 3-family homes often exempt | W-2 staffing agencies and apps (Instawork under 6h, PeopleReady same day, Tradesmen about 24h; markup about 35 to 65%); sub-tier with GC written consent (days) | Prequalification (COI, EMR, ISNetworld etc.) about 1 to 2 weeks | Licensed electrical and plumbing work must be by the licensee's **direct employees**; union jobs; **1099 app workers risky** (NY Fair Play Act presumes employment) |
| **France / Paris** | Carte BTP: **provisional PDF instantly**, card in 5 to 10 days | **Intérim** (about 8% of construction employment; profiles within 24h; about 1.9× gross wage) | Subcontractor approval by the client under the 1975 law, payment guarantee, PPSPS safety plan (days to weeks) | For-profit lending illegal outside temp agencies; at-cost lending legal but needs worker consent, an inter-company agreement, a contract amendment and works council (CSE) consultation; labour-only "subcontract" = criminal offence (délit de marchandage) |
| **Dubai / UAE** | Site induction required; no city-wide worker card found | **Licensed manpower suppliers** (48 to 72h; about AED 2.8k to 3.5k per worker-month, *unverified*); **MOHRE temporary work permit** to second a worker for up to 6 months (about 3 to 7 working days, sponsor NOC) | Subcontractor registration (Dubai Municipality, Law 7/2025) plus consultant approval: 1 to 4+ weeks | Unpermitted work: fines AED 100k to 1M |
| **Hong Kong** | Construction worker registration ("no registration, no work") plus Green Card: about 2 to 3 weeks from scratch | Registered workers via subcontractors or gang leaders (daily-rated, same day to a few days); engaging an **already-registered** subcontractor | New specialist-trade (RSTCS) registration: weeks to months | Designated skilled trades need the registered trade division; public works require registered subcontractors at every tier; imported labour tied to employer and project (5+ months) |

## 3. Platforms and business models (what works)

| Pattern | Examples | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Employer-of-record staffing** (agency employs, carries workers' comp and credentials) | Instawork (about 35% markup), Tradesmen International, PeopleReady, Labor Finders; UK agencies; French intérim majors | **Works.** Sustainable 25 to 35%+ margins. |
| Horizontal gig platform entering skilled construction | Workrise (valued $2.9B in 2021, exited construction in 2022) | **Failed** in skilled trades |
| Peer-to-peer **equipment** sharing between contractors | Yard Club (absorbed by Caterpillar), DOZR (receivership) | **Failed.** EquipmentShare scaled only by owning its fleet. |
| Contractor-to-contractor labour sharing | SmartBench (US, early), Sukedachi (Japan, 200k+ businesses, trade matching) | Closest comparables. Worth deeper study. |
| Government lending rail | Ajeer (Saudi Arabia: lend workers without transferring sponsorship) | Shows regulators will build rails when demand is real |

Agency share of construction labour: UK about 9% agency plus about a third self-employed; France about 8% intérim (about 3× the all-sector rate); US temp-agency share low overall (0.6% of all employment), with construction over-represented among contract workers.

## 4. Corrections to earlier claims

1. "Short-notice moves are viable only where the sub already has a relationship with both GCs (L2)" is **too narrow**. Fast legal routes also include: the destination's **approved sub takes on agency labour**; a **sub-tier under the destination's approved sub** (with written consent); labour-only gangs (UK, HK); temporary work permits (UAE); intérim (France).
2. "Equipment sharing is the easiest" was **legally true but commercially wrong**. Peer-to-peer equipment marketplaces failed. Integrate with rental companies instead of building supply.
3. **Credentials are portable and belong to the worker**, so a pool of pre-credentialed workers is the core asset. The bottleneck is **contracts and approvals between companies**, not the law on workers.

## 5. Implications for SiteSync

- **Don't become the employer or a labour marketplace.** Agencies and manpower suppliers already carry employment, insurance and credentials profitably.
- **Sell the 1 to 3 week prediction.** Nobody else has forward demand and idle signals. Buyers: developers' monitoring and PM consultancies and lenders (core), GCs, and **agencies and manpower suppliers, who value advance demand**.
- **A new revenue line that avoids being the employer: referral and partner fees** from agencies, manpower suppliers and rental companies when a predicted need is filled through them.
- **Fulfilment options per need** (the next-best-action engine chooses, per market): (1) slot swap with the same sub; (2) sub-tier under the destination's approved sub; (3) approved sub adds agency labour; (4) temporary permit or at-cost loan under pre-signed frameworks (UAE, France); (5) equipment through rental partners.
- **Per-market compliance checklist** in the product (credential valid, right to work, permit or consent, licensed-trade rule, union status). Never propose a blocked route.
- **Pre-cleared pools** (stage 2): workers' credentials verified in advance; France framework agreements with standing works-council consultation; UAE NOCs pre-arranged. This turns days into hours.
