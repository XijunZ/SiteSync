The problem

Construction sites don’t fail alone.

Every project runs on a chain of trades. When one link slips, everything booked after it falls out of sync, on that site and on others.

Material failuresA batch fails QA, a delivery is late
WeatherRain, wind or frost stops weather-sensitive work
Late inspections & approvalsBuilding control, utilities, permits
Labour no-showsSubcontractors overbook and pull crews
Design changesLate information, rework
Equipment downHoists, cranes, plant
Idle crewspaid to wait when their work isn’t ready
Shortagesa site nearby is short of the same trade
Late handoverssite overheads + delay damages, every day
Found out too latethe morning the crew turns up; companies never share schedules

Tech stack

Capture. Understand. Keep in sync.

Capture

Plaud

  • Site managers talk, they don’t fill in forms
  • Voice notes and supplier calls, pulled from the Plaud cloud
  • Speaker-labelled transcripts
Without it: updates stay in people’s heads until it’s too late.
Understand

Crusoe

  • gpt-oss-120b turns a rambling note into a schedule change in ~1 s
  • Kimi K2.6 vision for labour-plan photos
  • Open-weight models, hosted on Crusoe
Why it matters: competitors’ schedules never go to a closed-model vendor, so they can safely share.
Keep in sync

Neo4j

  • Every project is a dependency graph
  • A delay is a path: roof → weathertight → first fix → handover
  • A fix is a pattern: same subcontractor, approved at both companies, within 10 km
Without it: cross-company matching is a spreadsheet nobody can maintain.
Plaud note→Crusoe extracts→Neo4j propagates & matches→Sync Board
